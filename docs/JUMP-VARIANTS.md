# Jump variants and power work

**Status:** implemented 2026-10-31 (`DEC-POWER-04`).

## Current prescription

| Session | Exercise | Dose | Stop rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Squat emphasis | Box Jump | 3 × 3–5 | Maximal intent; full recovery; stop when jump height or quality drops |
| Hinge emphasis | Broad Jump | 3 × 3–5 | Maximal intent; full recovery; stop when distance or landing quality drops |

Power work is performed before the main lift, with no RIR target, e1RM, external load or double progression. Readiness and deload may reduce sets; they do not invent an RIR for a quality item.

## Why a horizontal jump complements a box jump

Box jumps are predominantly vertical and land on an elevated surface. Broad jumps add:

- a horizontal force vector;
- a normal ground landing and longer landing exposure;
- a different expression of lower-body power without external axial loading.

That makes broad jump a useful complement rather than a duplicate vertical jump.

## Evidence

### Vertical versus horizontal plyometric training

Moran et al. compared vertically and horizontally oriented plyometric training in a systematic review and meta-analysis. Horizontally oriented training had a moderate advantage for horizontal outcomes (ES 0.65, 95% CI 0.12–1.18). For vertical outcomes the difference between orientations was trivial and non-significant (-0.04, 95% CI -0.33–0.24). Both orientations improved performance.

- Moran J, Ramirez-Campillo R, Liew B, Chaabene H, Behm DG, García-Hermoso A, Izquierdo M, Granacher U. Effects of Vertically and Horizontally Orientated Plyometric Training on Physical Performance: A Meta-analytical Comparison. *Sports Med.* 2021;51(1):65–79. DOI: [10.1007/s40279-020-01340-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-020-01340-6)

**Interpretation:** a broad jump adds a horizontal-specific stimulus without evidence that it will reduce vertical development. It is not proof that broad jump is superior to box jump for this athlete.

### Unilateral versus bilateral plyometric training

A 2026 meta-analysis in adolescent team-sport athletes found no clear between-group advantage for bilateral countermovement jump or standing long jump, but unilateral training was better for single-leg countermovement jump and single-leg change of direction.

- Zhang F, Liu Y, Shi S, Wang H, Liu J, Yeremenko O. Comparative effects of unilateral and bilateral plyometric training on physical fitness in adolescent team-sport athletes: a systematic review and meta-analysis. *Front Physiol.* 2026;17:1820331. DOI: [10.3389/fphys.2026.1820331](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2026.1820331)

**Interpretation:** unilateral hops are a reasonable future addition for single-leg control, but they are not required to complement the box jump and are outside this change.

### Dose and certainty

The exact 3 × 3–5 dose is practice, not a validated protocol. The strongest conclusion supported by the evidence is that low-volume plyometric training improves jump performance, and that horizontal and vertical orientations are direction-specific. Power training also has modest, lower-certainty evidence for physical function in older adults.

## Alternatives considered

| Variant | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Broad jump | **Selected.** Horizontal complement, bodyweight, simple to dose by quality. |
| Countermovement jump | Reasonable, but still vertical; overlaps the box jump more than it complements it. |
| Lateral bound / skater jump | Useful frontal-plane and unilateral option; not selected for this change. |
| Single-leg hop | Useful for single-leg tasks; higher balance and landing demand. |
| Depth/drop jump | Effective reactive stimulus, but higher intensity and landing demand; not a default addition. |
| Trap-bar or loaded jump | Adds axial loading; poor fit with the existing back-history caution. |
| Medicine-ball throw | Low-impact power option; not a lower-body jump and not part of this change. |

## Boundaries

- One quality-limited power slot per session.
- No additional jump is added to the same session.
- No jump-height or distance measurement is inferred from logged reps; the stop rule remains an instruction.
- The broad jump is bodyweight-only in the taxonomy and has no e1RM or double progression.
- This change does not add unilateral jumps, depth jumps, loaded jumps or a general fatigue model.
