### What the imbalance is

- Bench: **12 sets/week**.
- Squat: **7 sets/week**.
- Trap-bar hinge: **5 sets/week**.

This is a **strength-priority** choice, not a calculation error. Bench is the largest lift
dose; squat and hinge are lower.

## 2. Evidence relevant to the choice

- Resistance-training volume has a dose-response for hypertrophy/strength, but the mortality
  benefit is already above the 30–60 min/week range and plateaus. **Adding total lifting volume
  for longevity is not supported.**
- Strength is more specific than hypertrophy; the exact bench:squat:hinge ratio is not
  established by trials.
- The current total (57 sets/week when this audit ran; **63 since 2026-10-28** with the
  knee-flexion row, `DEC-KNEEFLEX-01`) is coherent for strength and above the longevity dose;
  the question is distribution, not “more”.
- Any increase in direct erector or hinge work would consume the last spinal headroom. All
  options below keep spinal cost at 3.

## 3. Options

All options preserve the four session types, the main bench exposure and the spinal cap.

### Option 0 — leave unchanged

- bench 12, squat 7, hinge 5;
- total 57; spinal 3; time ~151.5 min/week.

**Case:** bench strength is a priority and the current balance is deliberate. For a
longevity-first goal, this is defensible because total lifting dose is already sufficient.
**Cost:** bench remains the dominant lift; lower-body strength receives less volume.

### Option 1 — shift one bench-support set to the squat

Move one set from `bench_support` in the squat session to `squat_main`.

| change | sets/week |
|---|---:|
| bench | 12 → 11 |
| squat | 7 → 8 |
| total | 57 (unchanged) |
| spinal | 3 (unchanged) |
| estimated time | unchanged (both 180 s rest) |

**Case:** smallest balance correction; preserves total volume, time and main bench exposure.
**Cost:** one less bench-support set; may matter if bench strength is the priority.

### Option 2 — remove one or two bench-support sets

Remove one `bench_support` set, or one support plus one main bench set.

| variant | bench | total | estimated time |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| remove 1 support | 11 | 56 | −3.5 min/week |
| remove 2 bench sets | 10 | 55 | −7.0 min/week |

**Case:** pressing volume is considered redundant and recovery/time is tight.
**Cost:** reduces a lift that is currently prioritized; no direct longevity benefit.

### Option 3 — add three low-axial lower-body sets

Add three sets of single-leg/split-squat work at 90 s rest.

| change | value |
|---|---:|
| total sets | 57 → 60 |
| spinal cost | 3 (unchanged) |
| estimated time | +6 min/week |
| per-session time | still under the 80-minute planned cap |

**Case:** more unilateral/hip support without consuming the spinal cap.
**Cost:** more total lifting volume, which longevity evidence does **not** require; may
displace recovery or cardio.

### Option 4 — shift volume toward power/balance instead of lifting

Not a set change inside the current templates: add or prioritise a small power/balance dose
(already partly present via box jumps) and keep lifting volume unchanged.

**Case:** longevity/function priority; power and balance are cheaper additions than more sets.
**Cost:** outside the current W8 set ledger; needs a separate programme decision.

## 4. Spinal and time constraints

- All options keep the counted spinal cost at **3**; none adds a direct erector or heavy hinge
  exposure.
- Option 3 adds ~6 min/week; at ~50.5 min/session plus warm-ups, it remains under the 80-minute
  planned cap.
- Option 1 is time-neutral; Option 2 saves time.
- None of the options creates headroom in the spinal cap; that remains a separate W9 issue.

## 5. Recommendation

**No change now.** The current split is coherent for a strength-first athlete, and longevity
does not justify more lifting volume.

If the athlete wants a better lower-body/upper-body balance:

- **Option 1** is the smallest defensible change: one bench-support set → one squat set;
- **Option 2** only if pressing volume is genuinely redundant;
- **Option 3** only with a separate recovery/cardio check;
- **Option 4** is the better longevity lever, but it is not a set-shift inside W8.

This is a user-owned programming choice, not an evidence-derived optimum. No template was
changed.

- The choice is explicitly separated from the evidence: **yes**.
