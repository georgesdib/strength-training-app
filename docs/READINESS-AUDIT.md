# READINESS AUDIT — can the current readiness policy be validated?

**Status:** audit completed 2026-10-23. No policy change. Decision pending.
**Scope:** W6 readiness vs no readiness; descriptive association and data availability.
**Related:** [`READINESS-WEIGHT-EVIDENCE.md`](READINESS-WEIGHT-EVIDENCE.md),
[`ENGINE-AUDIT-PLAN.md`](ENGINE-AUDIT-PLAN.md),
[`IMPLEMENTATION-PLAN-2026-10.md`](IMPLEMENTATION-PLAN-2026-10.md).

---

## 1. Question

Does the readiness-modified prescription produce better subsequent training than a simple
no-readiness baseline?

Current readiness can:

- apply a load factor;
- add RIR;
- remove one set from accessories and, with `include_essential: true`, from main lifts;
- gate current/future prescriptions.

The existing weight comparison only compared variants of the readiness score itself. It did
not compare the readiness policy with **no readiness policy**.

---

## 2. What the current data can and cannot answer

### Can answer

Descriptive associations between readiness and observed session outcomes:

- performance ratio;
- sRPE;
- RIR compliance;
- current data coverage.

### Cannot answer

A true counterfactual:

```text
same athlete, same date, same history
with readiness modification
vs
without readiness modification
```

because:

- historical sessions were imported, not generated by this app;
- the athlete did not follow two alternative prescriptions;
- the exported log has no record of a readiness decision;
- `prescription_modifier` only exists from 2026-10-23 and only for CLI-stored
  prescriptions;
- the readiness gate means the no-readiness path was never observed prospectively.

A simulated load difference is possible; a simulated **outcome** is not. Do not present one
as validation.

## 3. Descriptive results

Current database at audit time:

```text
sessions:                 373
ready readiness scores:   373
readiness < 0.70:          80
readiness < 0.60:          16
readiness < 0.50:           2
sessions with performance ratio: 349
symptom readings:           0
```

### Readiness and performance

| group | n with ratio | median performance ratio |
|---|---:|---:|
| readiness < 0.70 | 73 | 0.962 |
| readiness ≥ 0.70 | 276 | 0.980 |

Overall rank association:

```text
Spearman rho ≈ 0.118 (n=349)
```

The existing late-window result is much weaker:

```text
rho ≈ -0.004 (n=100)
```

These are associations, not effects. The low-readiness group is slightly lower on the
performance ratio, but the direction is not stable across the full and late windows.

### Other descriptive outcomes

| outcome | readiness < 0.70 | readiness ≥ 0.70 |
|---|---:|---:|
| mean sRPE | 7.32 (n=44) | 7.12 (n=129) |
| mean RIR compliance | 0.862 (n=80) | 0.866 (n=293) |
| symptom readings | 0 | 0 |

The sRPE and RIR differences are small. There are no symptom readings, so the back-outcome
side is **not assessable** from this database.

### What this does not show

- It does not show that readiness reductions harmed performance.
- It does not show that they helped.
- It does not show that the gate is unnecessary.
- It does not show that the gate is necessary.
- It does not support changing weights or cut-points.

## 4. Findings

### F1 — The no-readiness baseline is not identifiable retrospectively

**Classification:** confirmed audit limitation.

A valid comparison requires the outcome under both policies. The current log contains only one
observed training path, and historical sessions were imported rather than generated by the
app. A simulated no-readiness prescription has no observed outcome.

### F2 — Readiness-performance association is weak and unstable

**Classification:** confirmed descriptive result.

Full-window rho ≈ 0.118; late-window rho ≈ -0.004. This is consistent with readiness being
context, not a strong predictor. It is not evidence for a specific threshold or weight.

### F3 — There is no evidence that the current readiness policy improves outcomes

**Classification:** confirmed absence of evidence, not evidence of absence.

No walk-forward outcome comparison exists. The policy remains a bounded, plausible safety
heuristic.

### F4 — Main-lift set reduction is an unvalidated policy choice

**Classification:** confirmed policy gap.

`config/readiness.v1.yaml` has `include_essential: true`; the engine can reduce main-lift
sets. `READINESS-WEIGHT-EVIDENCE.md` says “No automatic main-lift volume change from
readiness.” Those statements conflict.

This is a real training decision, not a reporting detail. It needs a decision record.

### F5 — The gate has not been evaluated for its consistency cost

**Classification:** design trade-off.

The gate blocks current/future prescriptions without a ready entry. The benefit is that every
prescription has a readiness context; the cost is a hard stop when logging is missed. The
current data cannot quantify that cost.

### F6 — Symptom outcomes are unassessable

**Classification:** confirmed data gap.

There are **zero symptom readings** in the current database. Any claim that readiness
reductions protect the back is unsupported by this dataset.

## 5. Policy options

No option is implemented.

### Option A — Keep the policy unchanged

- keep gate;
- keep load/RIR/set reductions;
- fix the documentation contradiction;
- wait for prospective data.

**Benefit:** preserves safety posture. **Risk:** keeps an unvalidated main-lift reduction and
gate friction.

### Option B — Keep safety floor, make main-lift reduction opt-in

- keep sleep/energy and symptoms as floors;
- keep load/RIR reduction, but make main-lift **set** reduction opt-in;
- keep the gate;
- add quick presets to reduce friction.

**Benefit:** removes the most consequential unvalidated volume cut without removing the
safety floor. **Risk:** one more user choice; may underuse readiness.

### Option C — Advisory-only readiness

- readiness displayed but no automatic load/RIR/set change;
- sleep/energy and symptoms remain hard floors;
- gate becomes a warning, not a block.

**Benefit:** maximal consistency. **Risk:** removes a safety heuristic that may be useful,
without evidence that it is harmful.

### Recommendation

**Option B is the smallest defensible change**, but it still needs user sign-off.

Rationale:

- the gate and sleep/energy floor are safety-oriented and cheap;
- main-lift set reduction is the largest unvalidated volume intervention and has a
  documentation contradiction;
- load/RIR reductions are bounded and reversible;
- no data supports removing the safety floor or changing weights.

Do not make the gate skippable yet. Quick presets can reduce friction without removing the
gate.

---

## 6. What would improve the evidence

A prospective natural-history record, not an experiment:

- record the readiness score and the applied modifier at prescription time;
- record whether the athlete accepted or ignored the reduction;
- record the next session’s completion, sRPE, RIR, symptoms and performance;
- collect at least 12–20 reduction events;
- compare with non-reduced sessions of similar readiness and training;
- explicitly report confounding and small sample.

No automatic weight tuning is allowed.

---

## 7. Audit conclusion

The current data **cannot validate the readiness policy** against no readiness. The descriptive
association is weak and unstable, symptom outcomes are absent, and the main-lift set
reduction is an unvalidated policy choice with a documentation conflict.

Keep the safety floor and gate for now. Decide separately whether main-lift set reduction
becomes opt-in. No implementation was performed by this audit.
