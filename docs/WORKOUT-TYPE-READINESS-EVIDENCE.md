# Readiness, injury and workout type — evidence and decision

**Status:** decision recorded. No automatic readiness- or injury-driven change of rep scheme or
session type.
**Date:** 2026-10-20

Question: should readiness, injuries and other metrics change the *type* of workout — strength,
maximal, volume, hypertrophy — rather than only load/RIR/sets?

---

## 1. Current determinants of the rep scheme

The scheme is chosen by the programme, not by daily readiness:

- base template: main lifts 3–5, accessories 8–12;
- weekly undulation when a block anchor exists:
  - week 1: main `strength`, accessory `accessory`;
  - week 2: main `strength`, accessory `volume`;
  - week 3: main `maximal`, accessory `accessory`;
  - week 4: main `strength`, accessory `volume`;
- `maximal` (1–3) is excluded on hinge lifts;
- no anchor → template ranges are used.

Readiness currently changes load, RIR and sets. Injury can exclude/penalise/cap. Neither changes
the scheme.

Evidence for the scheme groups: [`TRAINING-DYNAMICS.md`](TRAINING-DYNAMICS.md).

---

## 2. Evidence

### Periodisation / undulation

- Linear and undulating periodisation produce comparable strength and body-composition
  outcomes in a 29-study meta-analysis (Zhang et al. 2026,
  doi:10.3389/fpubh.2026.1707627).
- In older adults, traditional and undulating training also improved strength and fat-free mass
  similarly (Vargas-Molina et al. 2022, doi:10.3390/ijerph19084522).
- DUP is a variety/fatigue-management structure, not a proven superiority.

### Autoregulation

- Autoregulated load (RIR/velocity) versus standardised percentage prescription showed no
  significant 1RM difference (MD 2.07 kg, 95% CI −0.32 to 4.46, p = 0.09) in a 15-study
  meta-analysis (Hickmott et al. 2022, doi:10.1186/s40798-021-00404-9).
- Autoregulation is defensible for avoiding bad-day grinding; it is not evidence for switching
  the training goal on a bad day.

### Readiness monitoring

- Subjective self-reports are sensitive to training load, but heterogeneous and not a validated
  prescription rule (Saw et al. 2016, doi:10.1136/bjsports-2015-094758).
- Wellness indices track recovery/staleness, but no validated weights or cut-points.
- Perceived readiness can relate to performance in a small study (Munteanu et al. 2026), not a
  validated per-lift scale.
- HRV-guided training has small, inconsistent performance effects; HRV is context, not a hard
  daily gate.

Full readiness evidence: [`READINESS-SCORE.md`](READINESS-SCORE.md) and
[`READINESS-WEIGHT-EVIDENCE.md`](READINESS-WEIGHT-EVIDENCE.md).

### Strength and hypertrophy specificity

- Strength is specific to heavy loading: lower-rep, higher-load training produces larger 1RM
  gains than low-load training when effort is matched (Hammert et al. 2026,
  doi:10.1007/s40279-025-02370-8).
- Hypertrophy is similar across a wide rep range when effort and volume are matched; roughly
  5–30 reps, with low loads requiring more proximity to failure.
- Switching from a planned strength/maximal session to a volume session on a bad day therefore
  changes the stimulus, not merely the fatigue.

### Injury and pain

- Pain-guided activity modification is feasible in patellar tendinopathy (Sprague et al. 2021,
  doi:10.1186/s40814-021-00792-5).
- Exercise therapy helps chronic low back pain (Hayden et al. 2021,
  doi:10.1002/14651858.CD009790.pub2), but that is treatment, not evidence for an automatic
  session-type switch.
- Prior injury is a recurrence marker; it supports caution, exposure management and excluding
  provocative movements.

---

## 3. Interpretation

Readiness and injury affect **capacity and tolerance**, not the training goal.

- A bad day reduces the load you can express at a given RIR. It does not mean hypertrophy
  training is now the right stimulus.
- Switching to a different rep scheme changes specificity, volume and fatigue. The evidence does
  not show that this improves outcomes.
- Injury is different: it can make a movement unsafe. The correct response is to exclude or
  modify the movement, not to silently change the whole session’s goal.
- A maximal attempt on a very poor day may be unwise, but the evidence supports **not attempting
  it**, not automatically redefining the block.

The supported hierarchy is:

```text
injury/constraint veto or exercise modification
  > load/RIR reduction
  > set reduction
  > reduce/stop the session
  > change the planned rep scheme (last resort, explicit)
```

---

## 4. Decision

1. **Do not change the rep scheme automatically from daily readiness.**
2. Keep scheme selection planned by the block/DUP cycle.
3. Readiness adjusts load, RIR and sets.
4. Injury may exclude/penalise/cap exercises; it may not silently turn a strength day into a
   hypertrophy day.
5. If readiness is so poor that a planned maximal exposure is unsafe, the safe action is to
   **skip or downgrade that exposure explicitly**, not to invent a new session type.
6. Any readiness-driven scheme change should be a visible suggestion with confirmation, and
   should be evaluated prospectively before becoming a rule.
7. Do not use sRPE, HRV or recent stress to choose strength vs hypertrophy; use them for load,
   volume and recovery context.

---

## 5. What would change this decision

- prospective evidence that readiness-driven scheme switching improves strength or adherence
  without increasing symptoms;
- a validated readiness threshold for maximal-attempt safety;
- a clinician-defined rehabilitation plan that specifies a different stimulus.

None exists now. The default stays: planned scheme, adjusted load/RIR/sets.

No implementation was done.
